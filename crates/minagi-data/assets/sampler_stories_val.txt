Once upon a time, a slow old turtle named Shell lived near a hill where the children flew their kites. Every afternoon, the children ran up the hill with colorful kites in their hands. The kites rose into the air, and the turtle watched them with wonder.
"I wish I could fly," said Shell to a passing beetle. "But I am too heavy and too slow." The beetle shrugged. "You could always climb to the top of the hill and see the world from there." Shell looked at the hill. It looked very, very tall.
The next morning, Shell started to climb. He took one step, and then another. A rabbit hopped by and said, "You will never get there before dark." Shell kept going. A squirrel laughed from a tree. "That is far too long a walk for a turtle." Shell kept going.
Little by little, the ground grew steeper. Shell rested often, and he talked to himself. "One step is a small thing," he said. "But many small things are a long way." By the time the sun was high, he had reached the middle of the hill.
At last, in the late afternoon, Shell arrived at the top. The wind was cool and the grass was soft. He could see the whole village below, the river shining like a ribbon, and the kites dancing in the sky. "It is just like flying," he whispered.
The children found him there and gave him a seat on a flat rock. One girl tied a small kite to his shell, and the wind lifted it up. It floated above him like a bright flag. Shell smiled. He had not flown, but he had climbed, and that was his own kind of wings.
He learned that you do not need to be fast to get somewhere wonderful. You only need to keep going.

Once upon a time, a small gray mouse named Pim lived in the walls of a big library. At night, when the librarian went home, Pim came out and walked along the shelves. He loved the smell of old paper and the quiet that filled the room.
Pim could not read, but he loved looking at the pictures. There was a book with a painted ship on a stormy sea, and a book with a dragon who sat on a pile of gold. His favorite was a book about a garden, with flowers in every color.
One evening, Pim found a young girl asleep in a chair, with a book open on her lap. She had stayed too late, and the library was locked. When she woke up, she was frightened. "Oh no," she whispered. "I am all alone."
"You are not alone," said a tiny voice. Pim stood on the arm of the chair and waved. The girl was surprised, but she was not scared of a small mouse. "I am Lucy," she said. "I am trapped in here." Pim knew there was a little door at the back that the cleaner used. He led her through the dark shelves, and she followed his tiny footsteps.
At the back door, Lucy found the latch and opened it. Cool night air came in, and the stars were bright. "Thank you, Pim," she said. "I wish I could do something for you."
Pim pointed to the book about the garden. Lucy understood. The next evening she came back with the book and read it aloud, sitting on the floor by the wall. Pim listened to every word, and his whiskers trembled with joy.
After that, Lucy came every week to read to the mouse. Pim learned the words, and in time he could read a little by himself. He learned that a kind act can open a door in more ways than one.
